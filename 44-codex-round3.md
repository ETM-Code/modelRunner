## Executive Judgment

The full critic round changes the answer, but it does not overturn the basic pattern: the right play is still a services-first wedge with visible proof, recurring expansion, and a narrow enough ICP that one operator can build repeatable delivery. The difference from Round 2 is that Play 24 should no longer be run as a standalone "AI support deployment" agency. The best version is now a **support-led AI customer operations implementation practice** that merges Play 24 and Play 31:

> Start with AI support, returns/exchanges, and revenue-recovery workflows for Shopify/Gorgias/Zendesk/Intercom teams, then expand into Claude for Small Business / Claude workflow implementation only after trust and data access are earned.

This is not a cosmetic merge. Play 31 is too diffuse and too dependent on a partner-directory fantasy if run alone. Play 24 is sharper but risks becoming commodity platform setup if it cannot expand beyond support. Together they form a better business: Play 24 supplies the outside-in Loom wedge and measurable ROI; Play 31 supplies the post-support expansion surface, the Anthropic badge path, custom Skills/MCP work, and broader customer-ops retainer.

I would execute the merged play starting Monday, May 26, 2026. The honest base case is not USD 1M ARR in 12 months. The base case is USD 500K-800K ARR run-rate by month 12, USD 1M run-rate in months 14-20 with one delivery hire by month 6-8, and a plausible year-2 expansion above USD 1M if the offer becomes "own customer ops outcomes" rather than "install Fin/Gorgias/Claude."

## Should Play 24 And Play 31 Merge?

Yes. They are better merged, but with strict hierarchy.

Play 24 is the wedge. It has visible pain: slow support, repetitive WISMO tickets, returns, exchanges, warranty questions, refund leakage, subscription saves, poor escalation. It can be audited from the outside. The buyer understands the ROI in a week. It also matches the operator's proven preference for Loom plus personalized mockup outbound.

Play 31 is not the wedge. The critic killed the load-bearing assumptions: Claude for Small Business is not a USD 50-150/seat product; the partner directory is not built to surface SMB partners; the 10-person training requirement is real; the HVAC/trades ICP is aspirational rather than proven; and a Dublin solo selling Texas HVAC owners over Loom has a trust problem.

But Play 31 is still useful as an expansion layer. Once the operator has access to the customer's helpdesk, refund policy, order system, knowledge base, Slack, and internal SOPs, the obvious next question is: "What else in customer operations should Claude handle?" That is when custom Skills, Claude workflows, MCP bridges, Slack digests, finance/ops handoffs, and light CSB enablement become credible. The buyer has already paid, already trusted the operator with data access, and already seen outcomes.

So the merged offer should not be "Claude for Small Business implementation partner." It should be:

> "We reduce repetitive customer ops work for Shopify and remote-comfortable SMB teams, starting with support and returns, then expanding into Claude-powered internal workflows."

The Anthropic partner path is worth pursuing in parallel as a credibility asset, but it should not determine the first 60 days.

## Brokr.ie Versus The Merged Play

Brokr.ie is the more defensible company if it works. The workflow library, broker reference density, compliance-adjacent UX, WhatsApp intake, document classification, and renewal engine can become a real vertical platform. If the operator can get five Irish brokers paying in the first 90 days, Brokr becomes a very serious contender and may beat every services play on 24-month value.

But it is not the stronger single bet starting Monday.

The revised Brokr case has four hard problems:

1. **The legal wedge is weaker than advertised.** CPC 2025 gives digitalisation and vulnerable-customer air cover, not an AI-log mandate.
2. **The competitive response is today, not next year.** Acturis Predict and Unify mean the incumbent is already selling AI/data products.
3. **The addressable Irish paying base is smaller.** Realistic paying-customer TAM is closer to 200-400 insurance brokers than 1,200 all broker/financial/mortgage firms.
4. **The channel is less Loom-native.** Broker principals buy through events, compliance consultants, insurer BDMs, trade press, and peer referrals. That can work for an Irish founder, but it is slower and more physical than the operator's preferred outbound motion.

The merged support/CSB play wins on first-dollar probability, proof-loop speed, and breadth of remote-comfortable buyers. It can get real signal in 30 days. Brokr likely needs 60-90 days to know if the vertical is alive, and the first signal depends heavily on warm local intros.

My decision: execute merged 24+31 now; keep Brokr as the main productized-vertical fallback if the first 30 support audits do not convert and if the operator can secure broker warm intros quickly.

## The New Hybrid

The new hybrid is not simply "support plus Claude." It is **AI Customer Operations for support-heavy remote businesses**, with a revenue-recovery wedge.

Initial ICP:

- Shopify / Shopify Plus brands, USD 5M-25M GMV, English-language support, using Gorgias, Zendesk, Intercom, HelpScout, or similar.
- High repetitive volume: WISMO, returns, exchanges, subscriptions, sizing, delivery problems, warranty, product questions.
- Founder, ops lead, or CX lead can approve workflow changes without enterprise procurement.
- Second ICP after 3-5 cases: small SaaS teams using Intercom/Zendesk with repetitive onboarding/support tickets and a non-enterprise buyer.

Initial offer:

- USD 2.5K paid audit for borderline prospects, credited to setup.
- USD 7.5K setup for first five anchor clients, rising to USD 10K-15K with case studies.
- USD 2.5K/month optimization retainer for simple deployments.
- USD 5K-7.5K/month for higher-volume customer-ops deployments with reporting, QA, revenue-recovery flows, and internal Claude workflow expansion.

Do not sell custom agents first. Use the customer's existing platform AI where possible. The operator's value is workflow selection, KB/procedure quality, QA, escalation policy, reporting, and translating messy customer ops into a working system. Custom Claude or CSB work is the upsell after platform limits are visible.

## Why Not The Other Near-Winners?

AI Engineer on Retainer is still attractive. It is the most operator-shaped play and creates productizable technical IP. I rank it second because the first 30 days require prospects to grant repo trust or care about a public-repo PR, which is a higher-friction wedge than a customer-support audit. The buyer also knows they can buy Cursor/Claude/Devin themselves; the operator must sell judgment, not tooling. That is viable, but slower.

Capstan service-first is the long-term sleeper. If the operator values defensible product creation over year-one cash, Capstan may be the best 24-month play. But the immediate buyer pool is narrower, the security proof has to be more technical, and the USD 1M target likely moves beyond 18 months.

Aesthetic clinics are tempting because the mockup is so visual. The critic round makes the delivery risk clearer: cold IG is dangerous, real footage is required, EireDigital and Apaya are direct competitors, and churn is high. It can be a good cash business, not the best single bet.

RevOps is also tempting because the operator likes Clay/Smartlead/Apollo and the Loom demo is strong. The platform risk and outsourced-outbound skepticism now make it a GBP 500K-ish ceiling unless the operator is exceptional. It is a fallback, not the top pick.

## Execution Starting Monday, May 26

Week 1 should be entirely about proof and a narrow list.

Build three audit templates: apparel/fashion, supplements/beauty, and one small SaaS. Each template should show five flows: WISMO, returns/exchanges, subscription/account changes, product questions, and escalation. For each target, scrape the public help center, produce a five-question prototype, record a 3-5 minute Loom, and send a direct note to the founder/CX/ops lead.

The first 30 audits should be high quality, not scaled. No fake support tickets. Do not creep prospects out by manufacturing a support interaction with their team. Use public help-center content and clearly label assumptions.

The sales language should be outcome-based:

- fewer repetitive tickets,
- faster first response,
- better escalation,
- exchange/save flows instead of refunds,
- cleaner support reporting,
- less founder/CX stress.

The operator should pursue Gorgias/Intercom/Zendesk partner credentials only after the first paid case study is in motion. The badge is useful, but it is not the product.

## Kill Or Confirm Criteria

Days 1-30:

1. **Outbound wedge:** 30 high-quality Loom audits produce at least 6 meaningful replies, 3 qualified calls, and 1 paid audit/setup. If there are fewer than 3 meaningful replies or zero calls, the support wedge is weaker than assumed and should be narrowed or killed.
2. **Paid access:** at least one buyer pays USD 2.5K+ for diagnostic or USD 7.5K+ for setup and grants access to helpdesk/help-center/process docs within 7 days. If prospects like the Loom but will not pay or grant access, this is curiosity, not a business.
3. **Delivery leverage:** the first paid deployment can be scoped to visible value in under 40 founder hours and without custom data integration before value appears. If the first client needs 60+ founder hours or bespoke integration before any measurable outcome, the model must narrow immediately to a smaller workflow or a different ICP.

Day 60 confirm signals:

- 2 paid clients or 1 paid client plus 3 late-stage proposals.
- One reusable audit-to-setup SOP.
- A measured before/after from the first deployment, even if early: deflection potential, top intents, hours saved, refund/exchange save flow, or first-response improvement.
- One delivery contractor shortlist started, because USD 1M requires help by month 6-8.
