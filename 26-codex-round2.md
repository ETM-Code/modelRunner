The best version of the chosen play is not a generic "AI support agency." It is: managed Fin/Gorgias/Zendesk/Intercom deployment for Shopify brands and small SaaS teams with visible support lag, high ticket volume, and an owner/operator who can approve workflow changes quickly. Add custom Claude/OpenAI workflows only after the client has paid and the platform limits are concrete.

## Scoring The Four New Plays

I scored the four new plays against the same practical criteria I used in Round 1: cash velocity, credible path to $1M ARR within 10-15 months, fit with the operator's Loom/mockup channel, delivery burden, defensibility, and operator fit.

| New play | Cash velocity | $1M by 15 months | Loom/mockup fit | Delivery risk | Defensibility | Operator fit | Overall |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| AI Customer Support Deployment | 8 | 8 | 9 | 7 | 7 | 8 | 8.3 |
| AI for Aesthetic Clinics | 9 | 7 | 9 | 6 | 5 | 7 | 7.7 |
| AI Computer Use SMB Service | 7 | 6 | 7 | 4 | 5 | 8 | 6.5 |
| AI Ghostwriting for Founders | 7 | 5 | 8 | 6 | 3 | 7 | 5.9 |

AI Customer Support Deployment scores highest because the prospect's pain can be demonstrated from the outside. You can send a real support ticket, measure slow/no response, draft a better AI answer, and show a plausible agent flow in a Loom without needing database access. That is unusually strong. Most other plays require the buyer to imagine value. This one lets them see missed margin, delayed refunds, angry customers, and repetitive questions.

The main correction is that the play is less asymmetric after the first call. Deployment quality depends on messy, private assets: historical tickets, macros, help-center gaps, order-system integrations, refund policy edge cases, routing rules, tone rules, escalation policy, and QA. The Loom creates attention; the implementation creates or destroys the business.

AI for Aesthetic Clinics has perhaps the cleanest outreach channel of all 13. The buyer lives on Instagram, the pain is visible, and the mockup is obvious: send three reels, a content calendar, a booking-flow fix, and a simple "your competitor is doing X" audit. It also has faster first cash than support because the buyer already buys content and ads services. Its weakness is that it needs many more customers, churn can be high, and the market is full of marketing agencies that can quickly rebrand their offer as "AI."

AI Computer Use is the most exciting capability bet and the most dangerous operating model. A 72.5 percent OSWorld benchmark does not translate into stable SMB workflows at 130 accounts. The service would accumulate brittle automations, credentials, browser states, exception handling, and "can you just also automate this?" requests. It is a good technical wedge for a bespoke expert service. It is a poor first product if the revenue plan requires 100+ small clients.

AI Ghostwriting has decent first-dollar potential but is strategically unattractive. The platform risk is now explicit, differentiation is mostly taste and access, and the best public comparable supports "good income business" more than "urgent $1M ARR sprint." It is also less aligned with the operator's build-plus-sales energy than the technical services plays.

## Specific Critique Of AI Customer Support Deployment

The audit Loom wedge is genuinely asymmetric, but only at the top of funnel. It is asymmetric because it uses the prospect's own customer experience as the demo surface. You can expose slow replies, repetitive questions, missing help-center content, weak refund routing, and bad escalation from the outside. That is much stronger than a generic pitch deck. It also fits the operator's validated Loom/mockup channel.

However, it is easy to overrate. A Loom that says "your support team answered in 19 hours; our AI would have answered in 12 seconds" is not a deployment proof. Real support automation has to handle authentication, order data, returns, partial refunds, subscription edits, angry customers, legal language, product defects, VIP handling, and escalation. If the audit is too synthetic, it will generate curiosity but not trust. The wedge should be framed as "here are the top five automatable flows and a prototype of one," not "we can replace your support team."

The 28 clients x $5K-7.5K/month math is possible but aggressive cold from Ireland. It is not defensible as pure cold email with no reputation. It becomes defensible if three conditions hold:

1. The ICP is narrow enough that every audit reuses the same playbook: Shopify or Shopify Plus brands doing enough ticket volume to care, probably $2M-$20M GMV, with Gorgias/Zendesk/Intercom/HelpScout already in place.
2. The offer includes setup fees, not just MRR. A $5K-$12K setup fee screens for seriousness and funds delivery before retainers compound.
3. The operator hires delivery help by client 5-8. A solo founder cannot personally audit, sell, implement, QA, tune, report, and support 28 clients without quality dropping.

My base case is 15-22 clients by month 12, blended $3K-$5K MRR, for $45K-$90K MRR depending on tier mix. $1M ARR by month 12 requires above-average close rate, early case studies, and a high proportion of tier-2 clients. $1M by month 14-16 is the more credible target. That still beats most of the 13 plays.

Sierra and Decagon do not directly threaten the $1M-$20M ARR Shopify mid-market in 2026. Their gravity is enterprise: complex contact centers, large implementation budgets, security reviews, procurement, and executive transformation mandates. The more immediate threat is platform-native and agency-native: Intercom, Gorgias, Zendesk, Ada, Fin partners, and customer-support BPOs adding AI deployment packages. In other words, enterprise creep validates the market; platform-service creep commoditizes the implementation.

The single biggest risk that would change my call is not competition. It is that the implementation work becomes undifferentiated platform configuration faster than the operator can build proof and distribution. If Fin/Gorgias/Zendesk make "good enough" setup self-serve or partner-delivered, the premium service has to shift from "install AI" to "own support outcomes." That requires stronger operations, QA, reporting, and process redesign than the briefing currently emphasizes.

The kill criteria should be explicit. In the first 30 highly targeted audits, if the operator cannot get at least 8 meaningful replies, 4 sales calls, and 1 paid setup, the wedge is weaker than assumed. In the first 3 paid deployments, if implementation takes more than 60 founder hours per client or needs heavy custom integration before value is visible, the model should be narrowed immediately.

## AI Support Deployment Vs AI Customer Success

AI Support Deployment and AI Customer Success should not be merged on day one. They share tooling and buyer adjacency, but the wedge is different.

Support is reactive. The unit of value is a ticket resolved, a deflected contact, a faster first response, a reduced handle time, fewer "where is my order" tickets, better escalation, or lower support headcount pressure. It is measurable quickly. It can be audited from the outside. It has mature tools. It has obvious ROI language. It is the better starting point.

Customer Success is proactive. The unit of value is activation, adoption, expansion, renewal, churn prevention, QBR prep, account-health scoring, lifecycle messaging, and playbook execution. It is potentially stickier and more strategic, but it is harder to demo from the outside because the data lives inside CRM, product analytics, Stripe, HubSpot, Salesforce, Vitally, ChurnZero, Gainsight, or spreadsheets. The ROI also shows up over longer intervals. Salary benchmarks and CS software pricing show real budget in the category, for example Custify pricing pages around CS tooling (https://www.custify.com/blog/custify-pricing/) and SuccessLab salary ranges for CSMs (https://www.successlab.io/customer-success-manager-salary/), but the immediate outside-in wedge is weaker than support.

The practical route is: start as AI Support Deployment, expand into AI Customer Operations for SaaS clients after support access is earned. For Shopify brands, customer success is mostly support, returns, delivery, loyalty, and repeat purchase. For B2B SaaS, support and success merge after the first deployment: the same knowledge base, ticket taxonomy, product telemetry, CRM context, and customer messaging can power onboarding and churn-risk workflows. Do not sell the merged category first. Earn the right to expand.

## Missing Plays Worth Considering

AI BDR-as-a-service for funded AI startups is a tempting missing play because it matches Loom/mockup outreach. A funded AI startup wants pipeline, and the operator can show a prospect-specific outbound sequence, landing page, demo script, and enrichment workflow. The problem is that BDR services are crowded, deliverability is fragile, and founders are increasingly skeptical of outsourced outbound. It might work as a sharp niche - "we book design-partner calls for AI infrastructure startups" - but it is not better than support unless the operator already has a warm founder network.

AI competitive-intelligence dashboards are interesting but should be an add-on, not the main bet. They fit Sherlock's research muscles and can be demoed with a prospect-specific dashboard. But the buyer urgency is lower, the category is full of dashboards that become shelfware, and the path to $1M ARR requires product distribution rather than service cashflow.

AI customer success outsourcing for SaaS is the strongest adjacent missing play. It has budget, direct revenue impact, and a plausible retainer model. It loses to support as a starting wedge because the proof is private and the sales cycle is more consultative. I would fold it into the support play after 3-5 SaaS clients.

Trade-show / event lead processing is the most interesting missing micro-play. It has a hard deadline, obvious pain, and a concrete before/after: "send me your attendee list, badge scans, booth notes, sponsor list, and target account list; I will enrich, score, segment, write follow-ups, route to CRM, and produce call scripts within 48 hours." It matches Loom/mockup outreach well and could create fast project revenue around events. It is probably not a standalone $1M ARR business, but it is an excellent acquisition wedge into AI RevOps, BDR, CS, or support retainers.

One narrower variant of the support play may be better than the broad label: AI returns/exchanges/revenue recovery for Shopify brands. It would combine support deflection with measurable saved revenue: exchanges instead of refunds, subscription rescue, delivery exception handling, and product recommendation after complaints. That wedge may outperform generic "support automation" because it ties the AI deployment to revenue, not just cost reduction.

## Monday Execution Recommendation

The operator should execute AI Customer Support Deployment, but with a narrower beachhead than the briefing implies.

Monday's offer should be:

> "I help Shopify and small SaaS teams automate their top 5 support flows with Fin/Gorgias/Zendesk/Intercom in 21 days, with a paid audit, setup, QA, and weekly tuning until resolution rate improves."

Initial ICP:

- Shopify or Shopify Plus brand, $2M-$20M GMV, English-language support, visible ticket friction.
- Already using Gorgias, Zendesk, Intercom, HelpScout, or similar.
- High-volume repetitive support: WISMO, returns, exchanges, subscriptions, sizing, delivery problems, warranty, product questions.
- Founder/ops/support leader still close enough to approve changes without enterprise procurement.

Initial pricing:

- $2.5K paid diagnostic for borderline prospects, credited to setup.
- $7.5K setup for first 5 anchor clients, then $10K-$15K once there are case studies.
- $2.5K/month optimization retainer for simple deployments.
- $5K-$7.5K/month for higher-ticket deployments with reporting, QA, multi-channel automation, and revenue-recovery flows.

First 30 days:

1. Build a tight audit template and three vertical examples: fashion/apparel, supplements/beauty, and small SaaS.
2. Send 30 very high-quality Loom audits, not 300 generic messages.
3. Do not overbuild a custom agent. Use the target platform's AI where possible.
4. Track audit-to-reply, reply-to-call, call-to-paid-setup, setup hours, and first-week deflection/resolution improvements.
5. Kill, narrow, or double down after the first 30 audits using the criteria above.

The main operating correction is that this must become an outcome service, not a tool-installation agency. The client is not buying "Fin setup." They are buying fewer repetitive tickets, faster replies, better escalation, fewer refunds, and lower support stress. Every audit, sales call, report, and renewal should be framed around those outcomes.
